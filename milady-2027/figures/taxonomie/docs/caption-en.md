**Figure — Each constraint class calls for a mechanism that acts where the constraint holds: on each sample for C1–C2, on the distribution for C3–C4; projecting each sample onto a distributional constraint destroys the distribution.**
Blue marks what holds for each sample and the mechanisms that act on each sample; orange, what holds only for the distribution and the mechanisms that act on the distribution; grey, the data and the model. A filled dot is a sample (a discretized field **x**), an open dot a model output before correction.
**a**, Taxonomy of physical constraints; in the bottom boxes, "or" separates two possible mechanisms and "and" (C3) joins two conditions that are sufficient only together. **C1, local linear**: **x** belongs to a subspace *S* = ker *A* (∇·**u** = 0, ∇·**B** = 0, homogeneous linear boundary conditions); it is enforced by construction, with potentials (**u** = ∇^⊥ψ, **B** = ∇×**A**), or by the orthogonal projection *P* onto *S* (Leray projection). **C2, local nonlinear**: *c*(**x**) = 0, for example the residual of a stationary PDE (a per-sample constraint only if no time derivative is involved); it is enforced by a nonlinear projection, or by a penalty or guidance on the mean end-of-path prediction, which is biased: the mean of two admissible states is not admissible (Jensen gap). **C3, symmetry**: the distribution is invariant, *g*_#μ = μ (**B** ↦ −**B**, rotation *R*_π of VKS), but a single sample is not; an equivariant flow velocity field *v*_θ and an invariant base distribution make the generated distribution exactly invariant. **C4, distributional statistic**: stationarity identities 𝔼_μ[ℒφ] = 0, including the mean energy balance 𝔼[ε] = 𝔼[*P*]; they are enforced on the distribution, by a stationarity loss during training or by exponential tilting (I-projection) at sampling time. The zoom wedge links the C4 vignette to panel b.
**b**, For C4, three distributions of d*E*/d*t* = *P* − ε on the same axes. Left, the true distribution μ: spread out, with zero mean (triangle). Middle, projecting each sample of the model *q*_θ (grey dashes, shifted mean) onto d*E*/d*t* = 0 sends all the mass to 0: the mean constraint is satisfied, but the variance drops to zero and the resulting distribution, a Dirac mass, is singular with respect to μ (infinite Kullback–Leibler divergence), even for a perfect generator. Right, the distributional correction keeps the samples and changes their weights *w*_i ∝ exp(β d*E*/d*t*_i) (stems, self-normalized): the corrected distribution stays spread out and its mean is re-centered at 0; it is the distribution closest to *q*_θ in the KL sense under this constraint, close to μ without being equal to it (black dotted line). The bottom rows show seven-point samples: left, seven quantiles of μ, with equal weights; middle, seven quantiles of *q*_θ, all projected to 0 (the height of the rings encodes nothing); right, the same points of *q*_θ, kept and reweighted.
Illustrative curves, not measurements: μ = 𝒩(0, 1) and *q*_θ = 𝒩(0.6, 0.85²) in units of the standard deviation of μ; the corrected distribution is the exact I-projection of *q*_θ (β = −0.83, standard deviation 0.85); the weights apply to the seven centered quantiles of *q*_θ, with β = −1.11 chosen to cancel their weighted mean (effective sample size 4.1).
