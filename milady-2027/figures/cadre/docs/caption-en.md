**Figure 1 | Proposed generative framework: the physical parameters drive a latent flow matching, a potential decoder makes ∇·u = 0 and ∇·B = 0 exact, the symmetry B → −B is imposed by equivariance and the energy balance is imposed in law (on the distribution), not sample by sample.** Each constraint class of the theory note is handled at the stage suited to it (bottom strip): C3 (symmetry) by the equivariance of the velocity field, C1 (linear local constraint, zero divergence) by the architecture of the decoder, C4 (statistical stationarity constraint) on the distribution of the generated ensemble. The generated fields are intended for model reduction, data assimilation and reversal statistics. **a**, The control parameters **λ** = (*Re*, *Rm*, *μ*_r) condition the velocity field *v*_θ(**z**, *t*, **λ**), which transports a Gaussian noise **z**₀ ~ *N*(0, *I*) (*t* = 0) to the latent distribution **z**₁ (*t* = 1); *t* is the flow time, not a physical time. A single direction of the latent space is shown; the trajectories are drawn for the optimal coupling, which makes them straight. With the independent coupling of the flow matching loss, the exact flow in dimension 1 connects the same start and end points, but along curved trajectories (maximum deviation from the straight line: 0.47): straightness comes from the coupling, not from the dimension. The symmetric bimodal target distribution represents the two polarities of the field; the operator *S* (z ↦ S z, associated with **B** ↦ −**B**) swaps the two modes. If *v*_θ(S**z**, *t*, **λ**) = S *v*_θ(**z**, *t*, **λ**) and the noise is invariant, the generated distribution is exactly invariant (Proposition 4); assigning the upper mode to +**B** is illustrative. **b**, The functional decoder reads the latent tokens **z**₁ through cross-attention and can be queried at any point *x* of the domain Ω; it outputs potentials (**A**_u, **A**_B), and the fields are their curls, **u** = ∇×**A**_u and **B** = ∇×**A**_B, so that ∇·**u** = ∇·**B** = 0 exactly, whatever the latent distribution (Proposition 1; in a bounded domain, the potentials must also satisfy the boundary conditions). In 2D (2D MHD testbed), the potentials reduce to the stream function *ψ* and the magnetic potential *a* (**u** = ∇^⊥*ψ*, **B** = ∇^⊥*a*): the field lines are the contour lines of the potentials, closed or leaving the periodic domain, with no source or sink. Maps computed from random Fourier fields (illustrative). **c**, Target geometry: von Kármán flow in a cylinder (VKS type) between two counter-rotating bladed impellers. Left half-plane: poloidal streamlines of the schematic mean flow (two cells; fluid drawn along the axis toward each impeller, ejected radially, returning along the wall and converging in the mid-plane); black arrows: direction of rotation of the impellers. Right half-plane: lines of an axial dipolar field that closes outside the cylinder (the azimuthal component of the VKS field is not drawn). The model has not been trained on VKS: the internship validates the framework on simple testbeds, the 3-mode Gissinger model (E1) and forced 2D MHD (E2), with a kinematic 3D check (ABC dynamo, E3); the hollow arrow marks this target. **d**, Control in law: histogram of the dissipation *ε* over the set of generated samples. The raw distribution *q*_θ (dashed) has a mean that departs from the mean injected power; an exponential tilting (I-projection, weights ∝ e^{β·g} with g = *P* − *ε*, Proposition 7) brings *E*[*ε*] back onto *E*[*P*] without changing the samples. The other route is a stationarity loss during training (dashed arc toward *v*_θ), which also applies to values of **λ** without data. The balance d*E*/d*t* = *P* − *ε* is an identity on each sample, not a constraint: only *E*[*P* − *ε*] = 0 is one. A stationary distribution of d*E*/d*t* is broad and centred at zero (✓); imposing *ε* = *P* on each sample reduces it to a spike at zero, a distribution foreign to the true one (✗, Corollary 6). Illustrative histograms (tilted gamma distribution, raw mean 1.14 *E*[*P*]). **e**, Downstream uses: reduced-model bases, ensembles for data assimilation (members tightened around the observations), and reversal frequency from the Rice formula, *ν*₀ = *E*[|*Ḋ*| δ(*D*)], computed from the stationary distribution of the dipole moment *D* and from the dynamics *F*, without time integration (Proposition 8). All data glyphs in the figure are schematic, with no measured values.
