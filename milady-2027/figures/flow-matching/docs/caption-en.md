**Conditional flow matching: a velocity field *v*_θ(**x**, *t*, **λ**) transports Gaussian noise to the data distribution; it is the average of the directions of straight paths drawn between noise and data, it takes the control parameter **λ** as input, and samples are drawn by integrating its ODE in a few steps.** The straight paths serve only as training targets, and they cross; the sampler follows the mean field, whose trajectories are curved and do not cross. In all three panels, the horizontal axis is the flow time *t* ∈ [0, 1] (not a physical time; *t* = 0: noise, *t* = 1: data) and the vertical axis is one coordinate of the state **x** ∈ ℝ^*D*; the side profiles are the densities of this coordinate. One-dimensional toy example computed exactly with NumPy: base distribution 𝒩(0, 1), data distribution *μ* = ½ 𝒩(1.5, 0.35²) + ½ 𝒩(−1.5, 0.35²). The field drawn is the exact marginal field *v*⋆ of this example (perfect training assumed, *v*_θ = *v*⋆): no curve is a measured result.

**a**, Principle. A noise sample **x**₀ ~ 𝒩(0, *I*) and a data point **x**₁ ~ *μ* are drawn independently and joined by the straight path **x**_*t* = (1 − *t*) **x**₀ + *t* **x**₁ (grey lines, 34 pairs, 17 to each mode), travelled at the constant velocity **x**₁ − **x**₀. Because the coupling is independent, these conditional paths cross: through one and the same point (**x**_*t*, *t*) pass paths heading to both modes (dark fan: seven paths compatible with the marked point, *t* = 0.4, with endpoints **x**₁ at the quantiles of the distribution of **x**₁ given **x**_*t*; 72% of the weight goes to the upper mode, 28% to the lower one). The marginal field *v*(**x**, *t*) = 𝔼[**x**₁ − **x**₀ | **x**_*t* = **x**] is the average of their directions (thick arrow). It is the minimizer of the flow-matching loss 𝔼 ‖*v*_θ(**x**_*t*, *t*) − (**x**₁ − **x**₀)‖²: the network learns this average by plain regression on randomly drawn pairs, without ever computing the expectation. Small arrows: *v* on a grid, darker where the density *p*_*t*(**x**) at that point is larger. Insets: the same distributions in two dimensions (the vertical coordinate is the plotted one, the second is illustrative); an isotropic cloud for 𝒩(0, *I*) and, for *μ*, two clusters exchanged by the symmetry **x** ↦ −**x**, a nod to **B** → −**B** (the two polarities of a dynamo; the toy distribution is not an MHD field).

**b**, Conditioning on **λ**. The same network receives the control parameter as an input, *v*_θ(**x**, *t*, **λ**). The same ten noise samples (quantiles of 𝒩(0, 1)) are transported for two values of the parameter: **λ**₁ (vermilion) leads to a centered unimodal distribution, 𝒩(0, 0.45²), and **λ**₂ (blue) to the bimodal distribution of panel a. By analogy only, **λ**₁ stands for a dynamo below threshold (**B** ≈ 0) and **λ**₂ for a dynamo above threshold, with its two polarities. At fixed **λ**, the trajectories are curved and do not cross (uniqueness of ODE solutions); in one dimension, each noise quantile is mapped to the same quantile of the target distribution. Both distributions are symmetric: an equivariant field, *v*_θ(−**x**, *t*, **λ**) = −*v*_θ(**x**, *t*, **λ**), guarantees this symmetry for every value of **λ**, even without data (Proposition 4 of the theory note).

**c**, Sampling of the trajectory drawn thick in b (noise **x**₀ ≈ 0.13, **λ** = **λ**₂), with a vertical scale about 3.7 times larger than in b. The ODE d**x**/d*t* = *v*_θ(**x**, *t*, **λ**) is integrated from *t* = 0 to *t* = 1 with Heun's scheme, in *N* = 4 steps of length *h* = 0.25 (double arrow). At each step, an Euler predictor **x̃** = **x**_*n* + *h* **k**₁ (dashes, open circles) gives the point where the field is evaluated a second time, then the corrector takes the step **x**_*n*+1 = **x**_*n* + (*h*/2)(**k**₁ + **k**₂), with **k**₁ = *v*_θ(**x**_*n*, *t*_*n*, **λ**) and **k**₂ = *v*_θ(**x̃**, *t*_*n*+1, **λ**): two network evaluations per step, a second-order scheme. The end point (1.00) is close to that of the exact trajectory (1.05, fine integration); the predictor alone departs from it where the trajectory turns, as it chooses its mode (*t* ≈ 0.5 to 0.75). The number of steps needed therefore depends on the curvature of the trajectories.
