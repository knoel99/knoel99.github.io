# Caption — figure “vks”

**The von Kármán Sodium (VKS) experiment: a turbulent flow of liquid sodium generates an axial magnetic dipole that reverses at random, passing through a quadrupolar state.**

Key ideas. (i) Two counter-rotating impellers stir liquid sodium in a cylinder; the mean flow consists of two toroidal cells rotating in opposite directions and a poloidal recirculation. (ii) Through the dynamo effect, this flow generates a mean field dominated by an axial dipole; the 2006 dynamo was obtained with soft-iron (ferromagnetic) impellers, not with the stainless-steel impellers of the earlier campaigns. (iii) The dipole *D* spends long phases around +*D*∗ or −*D*∗ and switches rapidly from one to the other at irregular times; it also makes excursions (a drop toward 0 followed by a return to the same polarity). (iv) In the dipole–quadrupole plane (*Q*, *D*), the two symmetric states ±(*D*∗, *Q*∗) are connected by a trajectory that crosses *D* = 0 with *Q* ≠ 0: the reversal goes through the quadrupole. All the curves in **b** and **c** are qualitative; no values are given.

**a**, Cylindrical vessel (horizontal axis) filled with liquid sodium, drawn transparent; the impellers, which are identical (8 curved blades on the face turned toward the fluid; one is the image of the other under the rotation by π about a diameter) and set back from the end walls, rotate in opposite directions at frequencies *F*₁ and *F*₂ (grey arrows). Left, mean flow **u** (blue): arrowed ribbons, azimuthal rotation of each half, in the direction of its impeller; loops, poloidal recirculation in a meridional plane — the fluid is drawn toward each impeller along the axis, ejected radially, returns along the wall toward the mid-plane (thin ring) and rejoins the axis. The loops are contour lines of the stream function ψ = *r*²(1 − *r*²/*R*²) sin(π*x*/*X*), chosen for its topology and not computed for VKS. Right, mean field **B** (vermilion) of positive polarity: axial field lines inside the sodium, closing outside; they are computed for azimuthal currents distributed over the sodium volume, a qualitative source of an axial dipole (not a solution of the VKS equations). The layer of sodium at rest that surrounds the vessel and the motors are not drawn. The “dynamo effect” arrow indicates that the field is sustained by the flow, not imposed.

**b**, Qualitative time series of the dipole *D*(*t*): long stationary phases (turbulent fluctuations) around ±*D*∗, two rapid reversals (purple) and an excursion (green) that returns to the initial polarity. The small cylinders recall the direction of the dipole of **a** (arrow →: *D* > 0; ←: *D* < 0). In VKS, reversals were observed when the impellers rotate at different frequencies (*F*₁ ≠ *F*₂), with short switches (of the order of a second, close to the magnetic diffusion time) compared with longer phases of irregular durations (Berhanu et al. 2007).

**c**, Same simulation in the (*Q*, *D*) plane, with the *D* axis shared with **b** (dashes ±*D*∗): vermilion spots, region occupied during the stationary phases around the stable fixed points ±(*D*∗, *Q*∗) (discs), symmetric under **B** → −**B**; open circles, predominantly quadrupolar saddle points; dashes, slow curve of the model. The two reversals (purple, smoothed) turn in the same direction and cross *D* = 0 on the *Q* < 0 side, then on the *Q* > 0 side; the excursion (green) is drawn as an out-and-back path along the slow curve up to the phase reached in the simulation, without crossing the saddle point. The small cylinder ← | → represents the quadrupolar mode (axial field opposite in each half).

Curve method (qualitative). **b** and **c**: Pétrélis–Fauve normal form d*A*/d*t* = μ*A* + ν*Ā* − |*A*|²*A* + noise, *A* = *D* + i*Q*, with μ = 1 + 0.2i, ν = 0.5, complex white noise of amplitude 0.36 (time step 0.01, seed 7), running average over one time unit; window of 296 units chosen to show two reversals and one excursion. Parameters chosen by hand; neither the durations nor the amplitudes are those of VKS. Script: figure/data/make_data.py.
