**Thesis research plan: five testbeds of increasing complexity, three axes running across them, 36 months.** The thesis climbs a ladder of testbeds, from the three-mode reversal model up to the DRESDYN experiment, and advances three axes at each step: hard constraints, satisfied by every sample; constraints in law and symmetries, satisfied by the distribution of the samples; the dynamo threshold and reversals. Each axis leads to a module of the MilaDy toolbox, which serves model reduction and data assimilation. Steps 0, 1 and 2 extend the experiments of the M2 internship: the plan starts from results already obtained.

**Staircase (top).** One step per testbed, ordered from left to right by increasing complexity; the height of the steps is a rank, not a measurement. Step 0: reduced models of reversals, the three-mode Gissinger model (*Q*, *D*, *V*) and the DQVU model identified on the VKS simulations (Bousquet et al. 2026). The inset is a *D*(*t*) time series computed with the Gissinger model (μ = 0.119, ν = 0.1, Γ = 0.9); it shows three reversals of the dipole. Step 1: periodic 2D MHD. The inset shows the current density *j* (grey levels) and the field lines of **B** (contour lines of the potential *a*) of a reference snapshot from experiment E2. Step 2: periodic 3D dynamos (ABC and Taylor–Green flows, pseudo-spectral codes). The inset shows |**B**| of the kinematic ABC dynamo mode at *Rm* = 12 (experiment E3) on three faces of the box [0, 2π]³; the arrows recall the periodicity. Step 3: the VKS experiment in cylindrical geometry, simulated with SFEMaNS and LATU (the partners' codes, sources of data); the vessel and its two counter-rotating soft-iron impellers are drawn. Step 4: DRESDYN (HZDR), a cylinder rotating about its own axis, carried by a turntable that imposes precession about the vertical axis (dashed); the drawing is schematic, and the drawn angle between the cylinder axis and the precession axis is arbitrary. The "Internship results" row locates the internship experiments, run on CPU: E1 (Gissinger model) at step 0, E2 (2D MHD) at step 1, E3 (kinematic ABC dynamo) at step 2. The insets illustrate the testbeds; none of them is a result of the thesis, and no model has yet been trained on VKS or DRESDYN.

**Axes (horizontal bands).** Each label names what the axis newly brings at that step. *Axis 1, hard constraints (class C1).* Potentials ψ and *a* in 2D (**u** = ∇^⊥ψ, **B** = ∇^⊥*a*); vector potential **A** and choice of gauge in 3D (**B** = ∇×**A**, hence ∇·**B** = 0 by construction); then the boundary conditions: matching to the outer vacuum and jumps of the magnetic permeability *μ* at the VKS impellers, conducting walls of DRESDYN. The axis does not apply to step 0, whose state is a small vector of modal amplitudes, not a field. *Axis 2, constraints in law and symmetries (classes C3 and C4).* The symmetry **B** → −**B**, i.e. (*Q*, *D*, *V*) → (−*Q*, −*D*, *V*), and the stationarity identities at step 0; the mean energy balance 𝔼[*P* − ε] = 0 in 2D MHD; the helicity budgets in 3D; the *R*_π symmetry of VKS (rotation by π about a diametral axis); the centrosymmetry of the DRESDYN modes. *Axis 3, dynamo threshold and reversals.* The Rice formula, which gives the reversal frequency from the stationary distribution, without time integration; a lower bound on the error of a generator that is smooth in the parameter near a bifurcation, applied to the threshold *Rm*_c; the reversals of VKS; the threshold *Rm*_c as a function of the Poincaré number *Po* for DRESDYN. The axis skips step 1: Zeldovich's anti-dynamo theorem rules out a dynamo in 2D.

**Toolbox and applications (right).** The MilaDy toolbox receives one module per axis. It serves two applications of the ANR MilaDy project: model reduction (from snapshots to a few dominant modes) and data assimilation with TorchDA (an ensemble of trajectories narrowed by observations). The glyphs of the applications are illustrative.
