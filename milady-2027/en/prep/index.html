Preparation pathway

Eight modules, from first-year undergraduate (L1) level to M2 research level

This pathway leads from a first-year undergraduate (L1) level (calculus, linear algebra, some programming) to the level required to start the MilaDy internship: reading a paper on generative models under physical constraints, writing and training a conditional flow matching model, and assessing a generated MHD field against physical criteria. It comprises eight modules, about 215 hours of work and 121 exercises with solutions.

About These modules are independent notes. They are not an official course of ENPC, LISN or Université Paris-Saclay. Each module lists its sources; facts about recent research come from verified fact bases (the specs/sources/ folder of the repository).

The eight modules

Module Level Duration Exercises What it brings to the internship
1. Vector calculus and Fourier analysis L1 → L2 20 h 15 divergence, curl, potentials, FFT, Leray projection
2. Fluid mechanics and turbulence L2 → M1 25 h 15 Navier–Stokes, energy budgets, spectra, pseudo-spectral methods
3. Electromagnetism, MHD and the dynamo effect L2 → M2 30 h 14 induction equation, Rm, laboratory dynamos, reversals
4. Dynamical systems and invariant measures L2 → M2 25 h 15 bifurcations, chaos, stationarity identities, Rice formula
5. Probability, statistics and optimal transport L2 → M1 25 h 15 pushforward measure, KL and I-projection, distances between distributions, SDEs
6. Deep learning for physics L3 → M2 30 h 16 neural networks, POD and autoencoders, equivariance, PINNs and operators
7. Generative models: diffusion and flow matching M1 → M2 30 h 16 diffusion, score, flow matching, conditioning, FunDiff
8. Generative models under physical constraints M2 → research 30 h 15 taxonomy of constraints, projections, penalties, distributional constraints

In what order

Modules 1 to 3 form the physics branch, modules 5 to 8 the learning branch; module 4 links the two, because it explains why a stationary turbulent state is described by an invariant probability distribution.

  • Physics: 1 → 2 → 3. Module 3 uses the potentials and the projection from module 1, and the energy budgets from module 2.
  • Bridge: 4, after 2 and 5. It is used for reversals (module 3) and for distributional constraints (module 8).
  • Learning: 5 → 6 → 7 → 8. Module 7 requires the pushforward measure and the continuity equation from module 5; module 8 requires all the others.

A linear reading order that respects these dependencies is 1, 2, 5, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8.

How to work through a module

Each module follows the same structure: objectives, prerequisites, work plan (sessions, durations, levels from 1 to 3 that never go back down), chapters with definitions, theorems and proofs, executed Python code, graded exercises ★ / ★★ / ★★★ with solutions, connection to the internship, further reading and sources.

  • Read a chapter, then do the exercises listed in its work plan before reading the solution.
  • Run each code block and change a parameter: every block runs in under 30 seconds on a laptop.
  • Keep one page of formulas per module; module 8 assumes you can retrieve them quickly.
  • The interactive diagrams illustrate five key notions: flow matching, Leray projection, distributional constraint, reversals and the ABC dynamo.
